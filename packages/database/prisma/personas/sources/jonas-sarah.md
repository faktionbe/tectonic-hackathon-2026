## Gaps and risks KBC can spot

1. **Jonas has no hospitalization insurance,** and the baby isn't covered anywhere yet. Adding a newborn within the right window often avoids waiting periods, so timing matters.
2. **They have no family liability insurance.** This becomes much more relevant with a child.
3. **Their income will drop during maternity leave.** Benefits from the health insurance fund are lower than Sarah's salary and capped, so the household should plan for a few months of reduced income.
4. **Their savings are down €2,500 and monthly saving is paused.** They'd benefit from a clear budget for the leave period.
5. **The outstanding balance insurance** may need reviewing now that they will have a dependent.
6. **There's no child savings account yet.** Family often gives money at a birth, so it's useful to have one ready.
7. **Sarah has financial knowledge, Jonas not**

## How their app adapts

Pregnancy is the most sensitive life moment in this demo. **The app never says "congratulations on your baby" based on inference alone.** An inferred pregnancy is health-related data, a wrong guess is deeply uncomfortable, and a pregnancy can end unexpectedly. The adaptation works in two steps.

**Step 1: before confirmation (soft and neutral)**

* **Hero card:** "Big plans ahead? A free family finances check-up takes 30 minutes."
* **Neutral tiles** appear higher on the home screen: the budget planner, insurance overview, and savings goals.
* **An opt-in prompt:** "Is something changing in your life? Tell us and we'll tailor your app," with options like *new home, new job, growing family, retirement*.

**Step 2: after Sarah or Jonas selects "growing family"**

* **Hero card:** "Congrats, Sarah and Jonas 👶 Here's what helps before December."
* **A leave-period budget:** a simulation of their monthly income during maternity leave, with the gap highlighted.
* **One-tap actions:** add the baby to hospitalization cover, get a family liability quote, and open a child savings account ready for birth gifts.
* **Book an advisor,** with the meeting pre-filled as "Family finances before the birth".
* **"Why am I seeing this?"** says "Because you told us your family is growing," with an option to undo.

## Advisor view (the Scale pillar)

* The customer brief shows **"Possible life moment: family expansion (unconfirmed)"** with a lock icon until the customers confirm it themselves.
* Suggested opening: *"We like to do a family finances check-up every few years. Is anything changing for you?"*
* Talking points: hospitalization cover, family liability, the leave budget, and outstanding balance insurance.
* A note: *"Prefers in-person for big decisions. Offer a meeting in the Mechelen branch or a video call."*

## Tone and boundaries

* The tone should be warm, reassuring, and security-focused.
* **Never reference medical transactions** (gynaecologist, hospital) in the customer-facing "why" explanation, even after confirmation.
* Timing matters: act before maternity leave starts, not during the first weeks after birth when they're overwhelmed.
* If the pattern suddenly stops (for example, medical payments continue but baby purchases stop, or baby-gear refunds appear), **silently withdraw all family-related nudges**. This safeguard is a strong point to mention in your pitch.
